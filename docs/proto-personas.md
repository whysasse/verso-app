# Proto-Personas — Verso

Three archetypal users that ground all design decisions for Verso. Everything builds from this.

---

## 1. Knowledge Worker

**Alex Chen** — Product Manager, 34, Toronto

> "I don't just want to save things. I want to actually use them."

### Goals

- Build a connected knowledge base where saved articles become living notes
- Have reading flow naturally into his Obsidian vault without extra steps
- Process and annotate articles on the go, then revisit them at his desk
- Keep all content in open formats he'll own forever

### Frustrations

- Articles disappear into a "read later" black hole and are never touched again
- Proprietary databases that make it hard to export or migrate content
- Copy-pasting article text into Obsidian manually breaks his workflow
- Sync that breaks silently across devices

### Tech Comfort

**High** — Daily Obsidian user, comfortable with Markdown, iCloud Drive, and file-based tools.

---

## 2. News Enthusiast

**Sophie Lavoie** — Marketing Specialist, 28, Montreal

> "I just want to read without getting sucked into something else."

### Goals

- Catch up on long-form articles during her commute without distractions
- Have a calm, beautiful reading experience that respects her attention
- Save articles from newsletters and social media to read on her own schedule
- Keep a light archive of pieces she might want to reference later

### Frustrations

- Reading apps that push notifications or recommend more content mid-article
- Articles that look terrible on mobile — tiny fonts, cluttered layouts
- Opening Pocket or Instapaper and not knowing where anything is
- Paywalls that block access after she's already saved a link

### Tech Comfort

**Medium** — Confident with iOS apps; prefers things that just work without configuring folders or files.

---

## 3. Academic Researcher

**Dr. Elena Vasquez** — Humanities Postdoc, 39, McGill University

> "My sources need to be as organized as my arguments."

### Goals

- Save web articles alongside PDFs and integrate them into research notes
- Organize saved content by project or research thread
- Have a durable, portable archive that survives tool changes over years
- Read deeply and annotate without switching between multiple apps

### Frustrations

- Reference managers like Zotero handle papers well but ignore web content
- Dead links — losing access to a source months after saving it
- Reading apps that don't talk to the tools she already uses (Obsidian, Zotero)
- Cluttered, ad-heavy article views that make sustained focus impossible

### Tech Comfort

**Medium–High** — Uses Zotero and Obsidian; comfortable with structured workflows but not a developer.
